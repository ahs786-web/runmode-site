The problem

Running apps make things up. You can't tell which bits.

A recovery score with no visible inputs. A readiness number from nowhere. An AI summary that sounds sure about data it doesn't have. If you can't see what's measured and what's guessed, you can't trust any of it.

What you usually get

  • One score, no working shown
  • Gaps filled with something that looks right
  • A plan written for you that you're expected to follow

What RunMode does

  • Scores that open up into the parts that made them
  • "—" when the inputs aren't good enough
  • Your own plan, if you have one, next to what you actually ran

Closed apps are a black box. AI chat coaches fly blind. RunMode has your real data, explains every decision — and you can check the maths.

How it works

Three steps. No questionnaire.

  1. Connect Apple Health

    RunMode reads the runs, heart rate and sleep already on your phone and watch.

  2. Add your race, and your plan if you have one

    Date, distance, goal. Following a plan from a coach, a club or a book? Add it or import it. No plan is fine too. Everything gets anchored to the race you're actually training for.

  3. Check the working

    Tap a score to see what fed it. Ask the coach why. The answer points back at your data.

Your plan

Your plan, next to what you ran

No plan imposed. Bring your own, and RunMode puts what you planned next to what you ran, from Apple Health. Add a plan of up to 24 weeks day by day, or import it from text, photos or a PDF and check every session before it's saved. No scores, no red marks, no “missed”: when Health has no workout for a day, it says so. RunMode never writes your plan or moves a session for you.

Imports are read by Google's Gemini if you've allowed the AI coach. If you haven't, an iPhone with Apple Intelligence reads them on the phone; otherwise you add sessions by hand.

Sessions

Build a session, run it part by part

Start from a pace run, intervals, strides, hills, sprints or a long run with a fast finish, or from scratch. Set each part by distance or time, give it a pace, a heart-rate zone or just an effort, and add repeats and recoveries. Run it on your iPhone or send it to your Apple Watch, with spoken cues for each part.

Trends

The long view, not a single score

VO2 max, resting heart rate, how much your heart rate drifts on long runs, and your pace at an easy heart rate — trends you can watch build over weeks and months, not one number trying to sum you up.

Apple Watch

On your wrist too

Set up a run on your iPhone, with a goal, your race or a session you built, and start it from your wrist. The Apple Watch app shows the run live, including your pace against the target, takes you through a session part by part, and lets you lap, pause or lock the screen without breaking stride. In the Ring layout, you choose whether it shows your pace right now, over your last kilometre, or your average. It saves the run to Apple Health when you finish, with splits that show in Apple Fitness. It comes with the iPhone app, not as a separate purchase.

Questions

Straight answers

Can I download it?

Not yet. There's no public beta and no App Store listing. Leave your email and you'll hear when that changes.

When does it open?

There's no date yet, and we'd rather say that than make one up. It's coming to the App Store in the UK first.

What will it cost?

That isn't decided, so we're not putting a number here.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

RunMode reads Apple Health, so it works with whatever writes there. A watch gives it heart rate and sleep to work with. Without those, the numbers that need them show "—" until there's real data behind them.

Does it work with Garmin, Coros or other watches?

Only through Apple Health. If your watch's app syncs runs into Apple Health, RunMode can read them. That hasn't been tested across brands yet, so we won't promise it.

Will it give me a training plan?

No, on purpose. RunMode never writes a plan. If you already follow one, from a coach, a club or a book, add it to the Training calendar, and RunMode puts what you planned next to what you ran, from Apple Health: “Planned 10 km · Ran 7.4 km · Matched by date”. There are no scores and no “missed” marks. If Apple Health has no workout for a day, it says “No workout found”. You can move, swap or skip sessions, but RunMode never changes your plan by itself. The AI coach sees your plan only if you choose to share it with the coach.

Can I import a plan I already have?

Yes. Paste it, pick up to 6 photos or screenshots, or choose a PDF of up to 10 pages. If you've allowed the AI coach, Google's Gemini reads it: what you chose goes to Google, photos without their location or other details, and nothing else is sent. If you haven't, an iPhone with Apple Intelligence reads it on the phone itself, and on other iPhones you add the sessions by hand. Either way, you check every session before anything is saved, and RunMode keeps only the sessions, not the photos, file or text. Plans can run up to 24 weeks. They're kept on your iPhone and in its backups, so export a plan as a file if you want your own copy.

What leaves my phone?

When you use the coach, the training context it needs and your messages go to Google's Gemini API to write the reply. If you've allowed the coach and you import a training plan, the text, photos or PDF pages you chose go to Gemini too, so it can read the sessions. Photos go without their location or other details, and nothing else is sent with them. Your location goes to Apple WeatherKit for forecasts (or a race venue's location, if you add one, and what you type to find the venue goes to Apple's place search). No analytics, ads or tracking. The privacy policy has the full list.

Who's building it?

One runner, through RunMode Ltd in the UK. Every feature gets tested on his own runs first.
