RunMode — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

RunMode is a running and endurance-training app for iPhone, with a companion Apple Watch app. It is built around a simple principle: your training and health data stays on your device. This policy explains the few things that leave your device, who receives them, and why — in plain English.

The short version. Almost everything RunMode does happens on your iPhone and Apple Watch. Your Apple Health data, the runs you record, routes, personal bests, training plans, notes and coach conversations are stored and processed on your devices. There is no RunMode server or account, and none of this is sent to us. A few features send a limited amount of data to other companies so they can work: the AI coach and reading a training plan you import (to Google, only for people aged 18 and over and only after you allow the AI coach), weather (to Apple), and race-venue search and maps (to Apple). The only personal data we receive is what you choose to email us. We don't track you, we don't add analytics to the app, we show no ads, and we never sell your data.

2. What stays on your device

When you grant RunMode permission to read Apple Health, and when you record runs, the app stores and processes the following on your iPhone and Apple Watch. None of it is sent to us, and there is no account or cloud sync. It leaves your devices only in the ways described in section 3, or when you export or share it yourself:

  • Runs you record in the app, on your iPhone or Apple Watch — the precise GPS route (recorded while the app is open, in the background or with the screen locked; on iPhone a blue location indicator shows while it records in the background), heart rate, distance and time. With your permission each run is saved to Apple Health as a workout with its route, heart rate, its distance in short pieces (so Apple Fitness can show its splits) and an estimated calorie burn. RunMode never uploads your precise route — a run's route and heart rate only leave your device if you export the run yourself (a GPX or TCX file, including Send to Strava) through your own share sheet, share a route as a course yourself (a GPX file with the exact route, including its start and finish, its height where it was recorded, and its name, but no times or heart rate), or share a run card yourself (an image you share, save to Photos or copy). A run card's route is a plain line with no map, its start and finish are removed, and it shows heart rate only if you turn that on. Saving a card to Photos uses add-only access: RunMode can add the image to your library but can't see the rest of it. When you import a plan from photos, Apple's photo picker gives RunMode only the photos you pick;
  • Motion-sensor readings during a run — on your iPhone to flag a recording that looks like a drive or a bike ride, and on your Apple Watch for cadence, climb and indoor distance;
  • Workouts and routes (runs, rides, swims, walks) with their distance, speed, steps, swim strokes and active energy; running measurements such as power, stride length, vertical oscillation and ground contact time; heart rate and resting heart rate; VO₂max; and sleep;
  • Derived training information — readiness scores, training load, heart-rate zones, personal bests, route history, and which of your workouts match your planned sessions;
  • Anything you enter in the app — check-ins, symptoms, subjective feel notes, event/race plans, sessions you build, your training plans and Training calendar (sessions, notes, skip reasons such as “Unwell”, and days away), and your AI-coach conversations — and the readiness score RunMode saves from each day's first check-in. When you remove an event, its name, date and distance are kept for up to 6 months so the coach can tell an older chat it is off; clearing all coach conversations deletes them;
  • Your date of birth (or the age you set in Profile), sex and weight, used on your device to estimate heart-rate zones, the calorie estimate for runs you record, and guidance when you have not set values yourself. Your date of birth, exact age, sex and weight never leave your device. If you use the AI coach, it is told only your age range (for example 35–39), as described in section 3(a);
  • The RunMode Apple Watch app exchanges data (such as live run details and settings) only with the RunMode app on your iPhone, and saves its runs to Apple Health. It sends nothing to the internet itself, except that showing a map loads map images from Apple Maps.

Because this data lives on your devices, you control it directly. You can erase what RunMode keeps at any time with Profile → Delete all my data, or by deleting the app, and you can clear coach conversations at any time (Profile → Coach → Clear all coach conversations, or one conversation from inside the chat). Neither removes:

  • runs RunMode saved to Apple Health (delete those in Settings → Apps → Health → Data Access & Devices → RunMode → Delete All Data from "RunMode", or delete single workouts in the Health app);
  • copies in your iCloud or computer backups, until those backups are replaced;
  • anything you've already exported or shared yourself; or
  • anything already sent to Google for the AI coach or to read a plan you imported, to Apple for weather, maps and venue search, or by an earlier version of RunMode to Open-Meteo for weather, which they keep under their own terms (see section 7).

Delete all my data also clears what the RunMode Apple Watch app keeps of its own: its settings, the route you sent to it, a run waiting to start there, and a run cut short there that wasn't saved to Apple Health. The watch does this the next time it connects to your iPhone.

3. What leaves your device

Three features send your data off your device, each to a named company and each only to make that feature work. They are described in (a), (b) and (d) below; (c) explains past-run weather, which RunMode doesn't look up. Each company that receives your data gives it the same or equal protection as this policy states and as Apple's App Store rules require: Google as our processor under its data processing terms, and Apple under its own privacy policy. Separately, whenever you view a map on your iPhone or Apple Watch, Apple Maps loads map images for the area on screen, so Apple receives that area and your IP address, as with any map app. RunMode never sends Apple your route.

As with any internet request, each provider also receives your device's IP address in order to reply. We never send any account or device identifier of yours.

(a) The AI coach — Google Gemini (optional, only after you allow it)

Nothing is sent until you allow it. Before the coach sends anything, RunMode asks whether you want to use it and explains what it sends, for coach chats (including a training plan, if you choose to share it), for coaching notifications and for reading a plan you import (section 3(d)). Nothing goes to Google unless you tap Allow. If you choose Not now, the coach stays off and nothing is sent. You can turn the coach on or off at any time in Profile → Coach; once it is off, nothing more is sent.

The AI coach is only for people aged 18 and over. If RunMode doesn't know your age, it asks you to confirm that you are 18 or over before you can allow the coach. If your age in Apple Health or in Profile is under 18, the coach stays off and nothing is sent to Google (see section 9).

When the coach is on, your question and the training details needed to answer it are sent to Google's Gemini API, which writes the reply. Google provides the Gemini API to us as our processor, under Google's data processing terms. This covers coach chats and coaching notifications. Coaching notifications are short notes the coach writes when something in your training changes, for example your training load rising quickly. They use the coach too, so they only start once you have allowed it, and you can switch them off separately in Profile → Notifications. A coaching notification sends only the one fact it is about (for example your training-load ratio, your recent intensity mix, the dates of your recent hard runs, how long it has been since your last run, how often you usually run, your usual run days and time of day, your usual weekly increase in distance, or how many other workouts you did between two runs), not your wider data.

A coach chat can send, depending on where you open it:

  • your messages and the recent conversation (up to the last 16 messages);
  • your age range (for example 35–39), if RunMode knows your age from Apple Health or from the age you set in Profile. It lets the coach compare your numbers with typical figures for your age group, and the coach is told that your own measured data comes first. Your date of birth and exact age are never sent;
  • today's state: your readiness score and its sleep, training-load and symptom breakdown, your training-load ratio and trend, this week's training by sport and intensity mix, last night's sleep, your usual sleep and sleep target, and any symptoms, feelings or effort rating you logged today;
  • the run being discussed: its name and date, distance, time, pace and splits, laps, heart rate and heart-rate zones, elevation gain, cadence and other running measurements, best efforts, your effort rating, for a session you built or an interval workout its name, parts and targets and how each part went, and the temperature, humidity and weather condition (such as rain or wind) recorded at the run;
  • your recent training: recent workouts (activity, date, duration, distance, average heart rate), your last few check-ins (energy rating, body signals and notes), past races and their results, VO₂max, resting heart rate and personal bests;
  • your target event: its name, date, distance, goal time and pace, training block, any training notes you wrote for the coach, and the course elevation from a GPX file you imported (and the file's name);
  • your other upcoming events in Prep: the name, date, distance and goal time of each;
  • an event you removed from Prep in the last 6 months, only if the conversation mentions it: its name, date and distance, so the coach knows it is no longer planned;
  • your training plan, only if you choose to share it with the coach. RunMode asks once, after you save your first plan with the coach allowed, and you can change your answer at any time in Profile → Coach → Share my training plan. While it is shared, each message includes your Training calendar as the app shows it: this week's and next week's sessions and how each one matched your workouts, your earlier weeks as planned and as run, skip reasons (such as “Unwell”), days away, your own labels and notes, and the event your plan ends with.

RunMode never adds your name, email address, location or GPS route, your date of birth or exact age, your sex or weight, or any account or device identifier to what it sends Google. Only something you write yourself (a message to the coach, or a note or label in a plan you share), or a plan you import, could contain them, if you include them yourself. If your heart-rate zones are estimated from your age, the coach is told only that they are an estimate, not the estimated maximum heart rate itself.

The coach gives training guidance, not medical advice: it does not diagnose or treat any condition and is no substitute for a doctor.

Under Google's terms, your prompts and responses are not used to train or improve Google's models. Google keeps them for a limited period only, to detect abuse and for safety and legal purposes. The data does leave your device to Google, so if you would prefer that nothing be sent to a third party, leave the coach off. The rest of the app works without it, and every figure the coach uses, apart from a removed event's details and the comparisons a coaching notification is about, is also shown in the app itself. Google's handling is governed by the Google Gemini API terms and the Google Privacy Policy.

(b) Current weather, race-day forecast & race venues — Apple

To show current conditions and your race-day forecast, RunMode sends your device's location, rounded to about 1 km, to Apple WeatherKit. If you add a venue to an event, that event's race-day forecast uses the venue's location instead, also rounded to about 1 km. To find a venue, what you type in the venue search is sent to Apple's place search (Apple Maps), which suggests matching places; the name and location of the venue you pick are saved with the event on your device. This is handled by Apple under Apple's own privacy terms. See Apple's Privacy Policy and the Apple Weather data sources.

(c) Past-run weather — not looked up

RunMode doesn't look up the weather for past runs or other workouts, so nothing about them is sent anywhere for weather. A workout shows its weather only when Apple Health already has it (Apple's Workout app saves it there). Weather that an earlier version of RunMode looked up from Open-Meteo, a weather archive service (it was sent a workout's start location rounded to about 1 km and its date, with no account or identifier), stays on your iPhone and can still show; Profile → Delete all my data removes it. If a later version looks up past-run weather, we'll update this policy first.

(d) Reading a training plan you import — Google Gemini (only if you've allowed the AI coach)

You can import a training plan you already have (Prep → Calendar → Plans → New plan → Import from text, photos or a file) by pasting its text, choosing up to 6 photos or screenshots, or choosing a file (a PDF of up to 10 pages, a picture or a text file). If you've allowed the AI coach (section 3(a)), RunMode sends what you chose to Google's Gemini API so it can list the plan's sessions: the text, or the pictures, with a PDF's pages sent as pictures. Before you choose anything, the screen says “Your plan goes to Google's Gemini to read it: photos, pictures and PDFs as soon as you choose them, text (including a text file) when you tap Read the plan. Photos go without their location or other details. Nothing else is sent.” Photos, pictures and PDFs are sent as soon as you choose them; text is sent when you tap Read the plan.

Pictures are redrawn before they are sent, with no metadata, so no location, date or camera details go with them. Everything you can see in a picture or page does go, so crop out or leave out anything that isn't your plan, such as your name. Nothing else goes with it: none of your Apple Health data, runs, check-ins or coach conversations, and no account or device identifier. RunMode keeps no copy of the text, photos or file. The sessions you check and save, with the plan's own words for each, are kept on your iPhone as your plan (section 2). Google handles what it receives as described in section 3(a): it is not used to train Google's models, and Google keeps it for a limited period only.

Plan import follows the coach's rules: it is only for people aged 18 and over, nothing is sent unless the coach is allowed, and once you turn the coach off, no plan is sent. Without the coach, nothing leaves your iPhone: on an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, RunMode reads the plan on the iPhone itself (finding the text in photos and PDFs on the iPhone first); otherwise you add the sessions by hand.

5. What we receive, and what we don't do

  • We don't operate a server or account system. The only personal data we receive is what you send us yourself. If you email us (for feedback, which opens your own email app, for support, for a privacy request, or to be told when RunMode opens), we receive your email address and what you write. We use it only to reply and, if you asked to be told when RunMode opens, to tell you once. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in replying to you, or for privacy requests our legal obligation to respond. We delete these emails 24 months after our last exchange, and launch-notice requests once we've told you. Please don't send us health details we haven't asked for. Our email is hosted by Google (Google Workspace), which handles it for us.
  • We don't add analytics, advertising or tracking code to the app. If you've chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple gives us crash reports and overall usage figures that don't identify you.
  • We do not track you across apps or websites (the app declares no tracking).
  • We never sell or rent your data.
  • We never use your Apple Health data for advertising, marketing or data mining, and we share it with no one except to make the features in section 3 work (the AI coach only with your permission), or when you export or share a run yourself.
  • RunMode works out scores such as readiness, training load and heart-rate zones from your data, on your device, and the AI coach writes guidance from them. These are suggestions to help you train. They don't make decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects, and you're free to ignore them.

Google may process coach requests and plans you import in the United States and elsewhere. Google LLC is certified under the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, which UK law treats as adequate, and Google's data processing terms also provide approved transfer clauses where needed. Apple handles weather, map and place-search requests under its own privacy policy and transfer safeguards. Emails you send us are held by Google (Google Workspace), which may process them in the United States and elsewhere under its data processing terms and the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Email us for more detail.

7. How long data is kept, and how it's protected

  • On your device: for as long as the app is installed, unless you delete it sooner. You can erase it at any time with Profile → Delete all my data (section 2 lists what that doesn't remove), and coach conversations can be cleared in-app (Profile → Coach). Your check-ins (and the daily readiness saved from them), notes, training plans and Training calendar, saved sessions, coach chats and settings are stored on your iPhone with iOS data protection and are included in your own iCloud or computer backups (encrypted in iCloud, and on a computer when backup encryption is on).
  • Emails you send us: deleted 24 months after our last exchange; requests to be told when RunMode opens are deleted once we've told you (section 5).
  • Google / Apple (and Open-Meteo, for weather an earlier version looked up): according to their own retention terms (for Gemini, coach requests and imported plans alike: brief retention for abuse, safety and legal purposes only, as described above).

Security. RunMode keeps your data on your devices under iOS data protection. Route fingerprints, imported courses and crash-recovery files have stronger file protection and are kept out of backups. Everything RunMode sends travels over encrypted (HTTPS) connections, without any account or device identifier of yours, and each feature sends only the data it needs.

8. Your rights

Under the UK GDPR you have the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict or object to the processing of your personal data, and the right to data portability, and to withdraw consent. You can exercise most of these rights yourself: you can see your data in the app, export runs as GPX or TCX files, your check-ins, notes and coach conversations as a JSON file (Profile → Export my data), and each training plan as its own file (Prep → Calendar → Plans, open the plan, then ⋯ → Export plan). Sessions added straight onto the calendar, skip reasons and days away are in neither file. You can erase what RunMode keeps with Profile → Delete all my data (section 2 says what that doesn't cover). We don't hold your app data, so we can't access, correct or delete it for you, and Google (like Open-Meteo, which an earlier version used for weather) receives no identifier we could use to find your requests. For anything else, email info@runmode.app and we'll reply within one month.

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your data, please email info@runmode.app with "Privacy complaint" in the subject. We'll acknowledge it within 30 days. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at ico.org.uk.

9. Age

RunMode is for adults. It is not designed for, or aimed at, anyone under 18, and you shouldn't use it if you're under 18. The AI coach, and reading an imported plan with Gemini, are only for people aged 18 and over, under Google's Gemini API terms. If your age in Apple Health or Profile is under 18, the coach stays off, a plan you import is read on your iPhone or added by hand, and nothing is sent to Google. We don't verify age. We don't hold your app data, so there is nothing for us to delete; Profile → Delete all my data removes RunMode's data from the device (section 2 says what it doesn't remove).

10. Our website

runmode.app sets no cookies and runs no analytics. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and GitHub records visitors' IP addresses in its server logs for security, under GitHub's privacy statement. The website's "Get told when it opens" buttons open an email to us in your own email app; section 5 explains what we do with it.
